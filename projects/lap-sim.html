Point-Mass EV Lap Simulation

MATLAB · Vehicle dynamics · Accumulator sizing

Point-mass electric vehicle simulation developed to evaluate accumulator configurations and predict vehicle performance over Formula SAE endurance. The model couples longitudinal vehicle dynamics with motor and battery constraints to estimate lap time, energy consumption, voltage sag, current, state of charge, and cell temperature.

Overview

I developed the simulation in MATLAB to support accumulator sizing for Bruin Formula Racing's Formula SAE EV. Changing the number of cells in series or parallel affects pack voltage, available current, energy capacity, mass, voltage sag, and ultimately vehicle performance, making pack selection a vehicle-level optimization problem rather than simply an energy-capacity calculation.

The model represents the vehicle as a point mass and calculates its longitudinal performance around the track subject to motor, inverter, tire, and battery limitations. The resulting mechanical power demand is coupled to a battery model to track electrical behavior throughout the event.

I used the model to compare candidate accumulator configurations and evaluate whether each could complete endurance while maintaining acceptable current, voltage, energy, and thermal margins.

Model outputs

  • Vehicle speed and lap time
  • Motor torque and power demand
  • Accumulator current
  • Pack voltage and voltage sag
  • Energy consumption
  • State of charge
  • Cell temperature
  • Current-limited torque

Vehicle model

The simulation uses a point-mass longitudinal vehicle model. At each point on the track, available acceleration is determined from the forces acting on the vehicle and the torque available from the electric powertrain.

Motor torque is converted to tractive force through the drivetrain and is limited by the applicable powertrain and battery constraints. Vehicle speed is then propagated around the track to generate the lap-time and power-demand profiles used by the electrical model.

Ftractive = Twheel / rwheel

Fnet = Ftractive − Fresistive

a = Fnet / m

Battery model

The vehicle model is coupled to an electrical model of the accumulator. Mechanical power demand is converted into battery power and current, allowing pack behavior to influence the performance available to the vehicle.

Pack voltage is modeled as a function of cell voltage and voltage sag under load. As the event progresses, the simulation tracks energy removed from the accumulator and updates state of charge. Current limits can then reduce the torque available to the vehicle when the requested power would exceed the capability of the selected pack configuration.

Cell thermal model

The simulation also tracks cell temperature throughout the endurance event. Resistive losses generated by battery current contribute heat to the cells, allowing candidate pack configurations to be compared not only by energy capacity and performance but also by thermal loading.

This is particularly useful when comparing different parallel-cell counts because reducing the number of parallel cells decreases pack mass but increases the current carried by each individual cell.

Accumulator sizing

I used the completed model to compare different series and parallel cell configurations for the 2026–27 accumulator. Each configuration changes pack voltage, capacity, current capability, mass, and electrical losses, so the comparison was performed using the complete endurance simulation rather than capacity alone.

The analysis led to selection of a 114s6p accumulator using 684 cells, with a maximum pack voltage of 478.8 V and approximately 7.35 kWh of nominal energy.

Endurance simulation

A representative 114s6p simulation beginning at 95% state of charge used approximately 5.53 kWh over the modeled endurance event and finished at approximately 26.6% state of charge. The simulated lap time was approximately 70.8 seconds.

These results provided an estimate of the usable energy margin remaining at the end of endurance while simultaneously checking whether the pack could satisfy the vehicle's current and power demands throughout the event.

Model development

The simulation has been developed iteratively as better vehicle and battery data became available. Rather than treating the lap model and accumulator model independently, I progressively coupled the electrical constraints back into the vehicle model so that battery limitations can directly affect available torque and predicted performance.

The model is continuing to evolve as measured vehicle and cell data become available for comparison with simulation results.
