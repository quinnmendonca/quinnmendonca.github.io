HV Isolated Backplane PCB

2026–27 Formula SAE EV · Accumulator electronics integration

Backplane PCB for the accumulator electronics stack, designed to distribute low-voltage power and control signals between boards that interface with the high-voltage tractive system while keeping the backplane itself isolated from tractive-system voltage.

Overview

The HV Isolated Backplane PCB provides the electrical interface between the PCBs in the accumulator electronics stack. Although the boards connected to the backplane contain circuitry interfacing directly with the tractive system, the backplane is intentionally isolated from high voltage and carries only low-voltage power, control, and communication signals.

The board replaces a large portion of the point-to-point wiring that would otherwise be required between the accumulator electronics. Centralizing these connections simplifies the electrical architecture, reduces harness complexity, and creates a defined interface between the individual PCBs.

The backplane is designed around the mechanical arrangement of the accumulator electronics stack, so electrical routing, connector placement, board spacing, and serviceability were considered together rather than treating the PCB as an independent circuit board.

Main functions

  • Low-voltage power distribution between accumulator PCBs
  • Control and status signal routing
  • Communication routing between boards
  • Defined electrical interfaces between accumulator subsystems
  • Reduction of point-to-point internal harnessing
  • Mechanical integration of the PCB stack
  • Isolation of the backplane from tractive-system voltage

System architecture

The backplane sits at the base of the accumulator electronics stack and connects the individual control boards through board-to-board interfaces. Each PCB remains responsible for its own functional circuitry, while the backplane provides the shared interconnect required between them.

A major design constraint was maintaining the isolation architecture of the system. Signals originating from circuitry connected to the tractive system are isolated on their respective functional boards before reaching the backplane. As a result, high voltage is not routed through the backplane despite its location within the HV electronics assembly.

Interconnect and signal routing

The primary purpose of the board is interconnect rather than signal processing. Power rails, control signals, status outputs, and communication buses are routed between the appropriate boards through defined connectors.

Moving these connections onto a PCB reduces the number of discrete wires and connectors inside the accumulator and makes the interface between boards explicit in the schematic. This also makes assembly and debugging more repeatable because signals can be traced through a documented PCB rather than through a point-to-point harness.

Design constraints

Unlike the HV Control Board, the backplane does not intentionally carry tractive-system voltage. Its main constraints instead come from system integration: fitting the accumulator electronics stack, maintaining the required isolation architecture, routing a large number of signals between fixed connector locations, and keeping the assembled electronics serviceable.

  • No intentional tractive-system voltage on the backplane
  • Fixed connector locations imposed by the PCB stack
  • Compact routing of power, control, and communication signals
  • Clear separation and organization of subsystem interfaces
  • Reduced accumulator harness complexity
  • Mechanical compatibility with the surrounding electronics

PCB layout

Layout was driven heavily by the physical locations of the boards and connectors in the accumulator stack. Because many endpoints were mechanically constrained, connector pin assignments and trace routing were considered together to reduce unnecessary crossings and keep the interconnect organized.

Ground pours and via stitching provide low-impedance return paths through the low-voltage system. Power and signal routing are kept organized by function, with communication and control connections routed between their corresponding board interfaces.

Mechanical integration

The PCB also acts as part of the physical integration strategy for the accumulator electronics. Board dimensions, connector positions, mounting locations, and clearances are designed around the complete PCB stack rather than around the backplane alone.

Using a backplane creates a modular electronics assembly in which individual functional boards can be installed and connected through a repeatable interface while reducing the amount of internal harnessing required around the stack.

Testing

Validation of the backplane focuses on connectivity, power distribution, signal integrity, isolation, and integration with the connected PCBs. Continuity and short-circuit checks are performed before installing the functional boards, followed by powered subsystem testing as the electronics stack is assembled.
